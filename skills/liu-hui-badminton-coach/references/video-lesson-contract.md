# Video Lesson Package Contract

Use the source video as the lesson container and one technique package as the teaching unit. One source video may yield several ordered technique packages and several pure-action episodes per technique.

1. Resolve the source video's complete semantic inventory before selecting frames. Record every title- or reviewed-ASR-supported technique with `lesson_topic`, controlled `action`, `family_id`, `taxonomy_path`, semantic interval, and `semantic_review_status`. Do not collapse a multi-technique video into one primary label.
2. Use the public title plus reviewed ASR topic indexes to decide what each section teaches. Use VLM frames only to reject non-action material and test visual compatibility; never let motion gating or free-form VLM semantics replace the reviewed technique identity.
3. Review at least 20 seconds of lesson context before the action and at least 20 seconds after it before approving an episode. Record `demonstrator_role` as `coach`, `learner`, or `unknown`; record `example_polarity` as `correct`, `incorrect`, `corrective_attempt`, or `unknown`. A coach deliberately showing a common mistake is still `incorrect`, and a learner repetition after one cue is still `corrective_attempt` unless the surrounding lesson explicitly accepts it as the target demonstration.
4. Publish a learner-facing action package only when a human context review records `demonstrator_role=coach`, `example_polarity=correct`, `context_review_status=agent_reviewed`, a context interval containing the complete action, and a short evidence summary. If identity or polarity is unknown, fail closed. Visual motion quality, a complete trajectory, or a high VLM confidence score cannot satisfy this gate.
5. Scan the whole source video for candidate actions. Reject talking, pointing, ordinary gestures, shuttle throwing without a racket stroke, grip adjustment, racket-face placement, isolated wrist or forearm rotation, and held poses. Do not keep these frames merely to fill a storyboard.
6. Preserve each admitted action as one continuous source-video episode. Do not assemble a motion route from timestamps belonging to different repetitions. A technique may retain several separate episodes when the video contains several clean demonstrations.
7. Classify every episode as `complete_demonstration`, `partial_demonstration`, `static_explanation`, or `concept_only`. Automatic motion/VLM admission only creates a candidate for human context review; it never certifies coach identity or correct-example polarity. Keep partial episodes and all learner/error examples in the private review workspace; do not populate the reliable action storyboard with them.
8. Tighten the action boundary before extracting teaching frames. For an admitted racket action, render exactly 7 strictly ordered teaching stages: preparation, start, loading, acceleration, approximate contact window, follow-through (including release), and recovery (including return to a stable ready state). The rendered frames are navigation samples inside one already-admitted continuous episode; they must not be used to manufacture missing stage evidence. Do not expand the action boundary into a following speech or explanation segment.
9. Store `start_seconds` and `end_seconds` as the strict action boundary. Store a separate `clip_start_seconds` and `clip_end_seconds` as the playback boundary. The playback clip may retain 1–2 seconds after the action to show shuttle flight or landing, but it must end before the next stroke and must not turn result footage into an additional action stage.
10. Keep every stage anchor and bounded stage clip inside its continuous action boundary. Use one representative frame per stage and retain alternates only in the private review workspace.
11. Attach a short `teaching_point` and `evidence_boundary` to every displayed stage frame. Explain what the learner should observe in that stage and what the image cannot establish. Do not leave an unlabeled gallery.
12. Present the playback clip before its stage storyboard and label both action and playback ranges. Stage frames are navigation and explanation aids; never present one still or three stills as the whole technique.
13. Do not quote or reconstruct course transcripts or subtitles. Store only short original topic summaries, controlled taxonomy labels, reviewed semantic intervals, and short original role/polarity evidence summaries.
14. Label exact shuttle contact, racket-face angle, force, grip pressure, true internal rotation, and calibrated 3D motion as unknown unless independently observable with suitable evidence.
15. Prefer `agent_reviewed` semantic identities and lesson packages. A model candidate may enter a review queue but must not be described as a reliable complete technique.
16. If a title-supported technique has no admitted complete coach/correct episode, keep the semantic section with `no_reliable_action_episode` and recommend a supplemental demonstration or retake. Never substitute a learner attempt, error contrast, partial model candidate, speech frames, or another technique's episode.

For full-corpus processing, run `scripts/video_lesson_batch.py` as an idempotent staged pipeline: inventory, prepare, gate, materialize, summarize, and publish. Keep downloaded sources, candidate media, raw model output, review decisions, databases, and logs in private ignored storage. Publish only short original summaries and `agent_reviewed` lesson packages; never publish private ASR text or model-only candidates as reliable teaching.
