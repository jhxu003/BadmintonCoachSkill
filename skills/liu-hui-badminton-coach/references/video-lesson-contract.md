# Video Lesson Package Contract

Use the source video as the lesson container and one technique package as the teaching unit. One source video may yield several ordered technique packages and several pure-action episodes per technique.

1. Resolve the source video's complete semantic inventory before selecting frames. Record every title- or reviewed-ASR-supported technique with `lesson_topic`, controlled `action`, `family_id`, `taxonomy_path`, semantic interval, and `semantic_review_status`. Do not collapse a multi-technique video into one primary label.
2. Use the public title plus reviewed ASR topic indexes to decide what each section teaches. Use VLM frames only to reject non-action material and test visual compatibility; never let motion gating or free-form VLM semantics replace the reviewed technique identity.
3. Scan the whole source video for candidate actions. Reject talking, pointing, ordinary gestures, shuttle throwing without a racket stroke, grip adjustment, racket-face placement, isolated wrist or forearm rotation, and held poses. Do not keep these frames merely to fill a storyboard.
4. Preserve each admitted action as one continuous source-video episode. Do not assemble a motion route from timestamps belonging to different repetitions. A technique may retain several separate episodes when the video contains several clean demonstrations.
5. Classify every episode as `complete_demonstration`, `partial_demonstration`, `static_explanation`, or `concept_only`. Admit an automatic batch episode only when one high-confidence, high-purity, semantically compatible repetition has a clear person, required racket visibility, a `full_action_trajectory`, and at least four visible stage codes without `no_full_action` or `incomplete_sequence`. Keep partial episodes in the private review workspace; do not populate the reliable action storyboard with them automatically.
6. Tighten the action boundary before extracting teaching frames. For an admitted racket action, render 7–9 strictly ordered stage frames spanning preparation, start, loading, acceleration, approximate contact window, release, follow-through, and recovery. The rendered frames are navigation samples inside one already-admitted continuous episode; they must not be used to manufacture missing stage evidence. Do not expand the action boundary into a following speech or explanation segment.
7. Store `start_seconds` and `end_seconds` as the strict action boundary. Store a separate `clip_start_seconds` and `clip_end_seconds` as the playback boundary. The playback clip may retain 1–2 seconds after the action to show shuttle flight or landing, but it must end before the next stroke and must not turn result footage into an additional action stage.
8. Keep every stage anchor and bounded stage clip inside its continuous action boundary. Use one representative frame per stage and retain alternates only in the private review workspace.
9. Attach a short `teaching_point` and `evidence_boundary` to every displayed stage frame. Explain what the learner should observe in that stage and what the image cannot establish. Do not leave an unlabeled gallery.
10. Present the playback clip before its stage storyboard and label both action and playback ranges. Stage frames are navigation and explanation aids; never present one still or three stills as the whole technique.
11. Do not quote or reconstruct course transcripts or subtitles. Store only short original topic summaries, controlled taxonomy labels, and reviewed semantic intervals.
12. Label exact shuttle contact, racket-face angle, force, grip pressure, true internal rotation, and calibrated 3D motion as unknown unless independently observable with suitable evidence.
13. Prefer `agent_reviewed` semantic identities and lesson packages. A model candidate may enter a review queue but must not be described as a reliable complete technique.
14. If a title-supported technique has no admitted complete episode, keep the semantic section with `no_reliable_action_episode` and recommend a supplemental demonstration or retake. Never substitute a partial model candidate, speech frames, or another technique's episode.

For full-corpus processing, run `scripts/video_lesson_batch.py` as an idempotent staged pipeline: inventory, prepare, gate, materialize, summarize, and publish. Keep downloaded sources, candidate media, raw model output, review decisions, databases, and logs in private ignored storage. Publish only short original summaries and `agent_reviewed` lesson packages; never publish private ASR text or model-only candidates as reliable teaching.
